title: Search and results
---

The legal research page is where you go when you want to search legal sources directly — browsing results, applying filters, and pulling what you find into your [projects](/guides/projects). Click **Legal research** in the sidebar to open it.

## How search works

Build a set of sources, type your query, and search across all of them at once. Use **Add Source** above the search box to pick which legal sources to include — they appear as chips under **Selected sources**. Remove a chip to drop that source, or add more to widen the search. A single query runs across every selected source in parallel, and the results come back merged into one relevance-ranked list with a badge on each card showing which source it came from.

A badge in the search box tells you which search mode is active for the current selection.

**Prompt search** lets you describe what you're looking for in plain language — something like "recent Dutch case law on unfair dismissal during probation." The source matches on meaning, not just keywords. The hint reads *Selected sources work best with prompt search*.

**Keyword search** is the traditional approach: specific terms, boolean operators (`AND`, `OR`, `NOT`), and exact phrases in quotes. The hint reads *Selected sources work best with keyword search*.

**Hybrid search** combines both — it matches on meaning and specific terms at the same time. The hint reads *Selected sources work with both prompt and keyword search*.

When your selection mixes sources that prefer different modes, the badge reflects the mode that fits the group. If you're not sure how to phrase what you're looking for, click **Improve prompt** before searching. The AI refines your query to help surface more targeted results.

## Narrowing results with filters

Filters appear in the sidebar and stack across every source in your selection. Date range is available for most sources. Beyond that, the options depend on what you're searching — institutions and document types for Rettsdata, fields of law for Moonlit and Rechtspraak.nl, tax type for Kennisbank, and so on. When a filter only applies to some of the selected sources, it narrows those results and leaves the others untouched.

When you apply a filter, it appears as a chip above the results so you can see what's active at a glance. You can stack multiple filters or clear them all to go back to the full set.

## Working with results

Each result shows what you need to evaluate it quickly: title, jurisdiction, date, reference number, document type, a summary, and a source badge so you can tell at a glance where it came from. Results are merged from every selected source and ordered by relevance, though you can switch to newest or oldest first.

Click a result to read the full document. For Rettsdata and Lovdata, the document opens right inside Saga in the side panel — no context switching. Other sources open the original document in a new tab.

If a result isn't relevant, dismiss it. If it is, you have two ways to put it to work. **Add to chat** drops the document straight into a new or existing conversation as an attachment, ready for follow-up questions. **Add to project** sends it to the [knowledge base](/guides/projects/knowledge-base) of a project you choose, where it becomes long-term context the AI can draw on in [chat](/guides/chat) and [grid review](/guides/grid-review). When an entire set of results is useful, **Add all results to project** saves the full batch at once. These actions work the same way regardless of which source each result came from, so you can add a mix of documents to the same project in one pass.

You can also use legal sources directly inside a conversation without visiting this page — see [Research in chat](/guides/legal-research/in-chat).
