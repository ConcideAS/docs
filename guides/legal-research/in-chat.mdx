---
title: Research in chat
---

Sometimes you're in the middle of a conversation — analyzing a document, drafting advice, working through a question — and you need to check what the case law says. Instead of switching to the [legal research page](/guides/legal-research/search-and-results), you can bring the research into the conversation.

Open the **Add sources** menu in the chat toolbar, check the legal sources you want, and send your message. The AI takes your question, expands it into search queries tailored to each source, and runs the searches behind the scenes. The results come back woven into the AI's response, with citations linking to the original material.

## The timeline

Below your message, a timeline shows exactly what happened: which sources were searched, what queries the AI used, how many results came back, and which specific sources the AI drew from. You can click any source in the timeline to read it — Rettsdata and Lovdata open in Saga's built-in viewer, while others link to the original document.

The timeline is useful for transparency. You can see whether the AI cast a wide net or a narrow one, and whether it found what you'd expect it to find. If something looks off, adjust your question and try again.

## Citations

Every claim the AI makes based on a legal source gets an inline [citation](/guides/quickstart#citations) — the same kind you see when the AI references your knowledge base or the web. Hover to see the original passage, click to open the source. Nothing is taken on trust; you can trace every statement back to where it came from.

## Deep research in chat

Deep research is a separate option in the **Add sources** menu, alongside legal databases and web search. When you enable it and send your message, Saga runs a comprehensive web analysis — planning multiple search queries, gathering results from across the internet, and synthesizing them into a structured report with citations.

The report appears as a document you can open in the side panel. The AI uses it as context for its response, so the answer you get reflects the full research, not just a single search result. The timeline shows the research stages as they happen: planning, searching, and generating the report.

Deep research replaces web search when both are available — you use one or the other for a given message, not both at the same time.

## Mixing sources

Legal research in chat doesn't exist in isolation. You can enable legal sources, attach a file, draw on a project's knowledge base, and turn on web search or deep research — all in the same message. The AI considers everything together. That means you can do things like compare a contract clause against both your internal guidelines and the relevant case law, without jumping between tools.

For more on how to control what the AI sees, see [Context and sources](/guides/chat/context-and-sources).
