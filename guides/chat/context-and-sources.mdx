title: Context and sources

The AI's answers are only as good as the context it has. Saga gives you several ways to control what information the AI can access — from a project's full document library to a single file you attach in the moment — and you can combine them in one conversation.

## Knowledge base vs. attached files

There are two ways to give the AI access to documents, and they work differently.

**Knowledge base** documents are uploaded to a project and processed in advance. Saga reads and indexes every page so the AI can intelligently search your entire collection on demand. When you ask a question, the AI decides whether to search across all documents for relevant passages or fetch a specific document in full — depending on what the question requires. This is the right choice when you're working on a matter over time and want the AI to have deep, persistent access to a body of material.

**Attached files** are added directly to a single conversation. You can upload a file from your computer or pull one in from any knowledge base available to you. The AI reads the full contents of the attachment as part of that message. This is better for quick, one-off questions — when you want to hand the AI a specific document and ask something about it without setting up a project.

In practice: if you'll ask multiple questions about a set of documents over days or weeks, upload them to a project's knowledge base. If you just need a fast answer about one file, attach it in chat.

### What happens when you ask a question in a project

When you send a message in a project chat, the AI has access to the names and count of every active document in the knowledge base. It then decides how to retrieve content based on your question:

- For broad or topical questions (overviews, comparisons, locating information), the AI **searches across all documents** and pulls in the most relevant passages.
- For whole-document tasks (full summaries, clause-by-clause reviews, structural analysis), the AI **fetches the entire document** by name.
- For questions that span multiple specific documents, the AI may **fetch several documents individually** when the full content of each is needed.

This means the AI uses your project's full context budget efficiently — retrieving only what's needed for each question rather than loading everything upfront. You can see the search progress in the timeline that appears while the AI works.

## Working with many files

Saga is designed to work with large document collections. In a project knowledge base, the AI sees the names of every active document and retrieves content intelligently — searching for relevant passages across all files or fetching specific documents in full, depending on your question. This works efficiently even with hundreds of documents.

When you attach files directly to a chat, Saga uses the same smart retrieval approach for large sets. Instead of loading every file in full — which would exceed the AI's context window — it searches your attachments for the passages most relevant to your question and loads those.

<Tip>
For the best results with project documents, use the exact document name when you want the AI to focus on a specific file. The AI can see every active document's name and uses it to target its search.
</Tip>

## Organizational sources

Organizational sources are firm-wide knowledge bases managed by your admin — things like standard clause libraries, internal guidelines, or regulatory handbooks. When they're available, a toggle appears in the chat toolbar. Enable the ones you want and the AI draws on them alongside your other context.

If you don't see the organizational sources toggle, your organization hasn't set any up yet. Ask your admin to create one from the [admin panel](/guides/admin).

## Web search and URLs

You can also bring in information from outside your organization. Enable **web search** from the toolbar and the AI searches the internet as part of its response. Only the message you send with web search enabled is exposed to external search engines — the rest of your conversation, your project documents, and any other files in the chat are not shared. Turn web search off for the next message and that message stays entirely within Saga.

For a specific webpage, paste the URL directly into the chat. Saga extracts the page content automatically, and the AI can reference it with citations linking back to the source.

## Legal research

Enable legal research databases from the toolbar before sending your message. The AI searches the selected databases as part of generating its response and includes the results with citations. A timeline shows the search progress, queries used, and results found for each database.

See [Legal research](/guides/legal-research) for more on available databases and search options.

## Drafting

When drafting is enabled, the AI can produce standalone documents as part of its response — a memo, a summary, a contract clause, or any other written output. These appear as downloadable files you can open in the side panel or export as Word documents.

Toggle drafting on or off from the toolbar. When it's off, the AI responds only in the chat. When it's on, the AI decides whether the request calls for a document and generates one when appropriate.

## Additional AI instructions

If there's something you always want the AI to keep in mind — a preferred language, a jurisdiction focus, a formatting convention — you can set it once and forget about it. Go to **Settings > Personalization** and add your instructions under **AI instructions**. They apply to every conversation you start, so you don't need to repeat yourself.
