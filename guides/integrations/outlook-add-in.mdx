The Saga Outlook add-in brings Saga into Microsoft Outlook.

## What you can do

The add-in opens inside Outlook. From there, you can save emails and attachments to Saga, bring project documents into drafts, summarize threads, and draft replies.

### Save thread to project

When you save an email thread to a Saga project, the entire conversation (including all messages in the thread) gets linked to a project in Saga. This means:

- The email becomes part of the project's record, alongside documents, notes, and other files.
- Anyone with access to the project can see the thread without having to forward emails or dig through inboxes.
- The thread is searchable and accessible directly from the project in Saga, rather than buried in your email client.

This brings relevant email correspondence into your legal matter workspace so everything is in one place.

### Save attachment to a project

When you save attachments to a project, you're moving files from an email (or chat) into the project's knowledge base. This action:

- Moves files from a single email thread or chat into the project's document collection.
- Makes the files accessible to any chat or grid review in that project.
- Stores knowledge base documents long-term so they can be used for multiple questions and analyses without re-attaching them.

### Summarize thread

Long threads accumulate quickly. Instead of scrolling through weeks of replies, you can generate a clear, structured summary of where things stand.

- Saga covers key points, organizes them chronologically, and rewrites them into a clean, easy-to-read bulleted list.

### Draft a reply

Saga analyzes the content of the current email thread you have open in Outlook, including the context, sender, and subject line.

- It generates a professional and concise response based on the thread's context or any specific instructions you give, such as "Draft a reply saying I approve."
- When you choose **Insert reply**, the drafted text is automatically added to your Outlook email composer, complete with the correct **To** and **Cc** recipients and subject line.

The email is not sent automatically. You always get to review, edit, and adjust the draft in Outlook before you hit send.
